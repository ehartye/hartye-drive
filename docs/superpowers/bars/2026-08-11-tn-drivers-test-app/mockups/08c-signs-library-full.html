Regulatory

7 signs

Red, or white with black letters. These are the law. Ignoring one is a violation, not a bad idea. The octagon and the downward triangle are used for nothing else on the road — you can identify both before you can read them.

Warning

8 signs

Yellow diamond. A warning tells you what is coming so you can change speed or position before you reach it — it never tells you what you must do. Pedestrian, bicycle and school warnings may be posted in fluorescent yellow-green; same shape, same meaning. One warning breaks the diamond rule: the no-passing pennant.

General warning

The diamond is the message. Adjust before you arrive.

Construction

1 sign

Orange, and only orange. Orange means the road ahead is temporary — the lane you expect may be shifted, narrowed, or gone, and there are people working beside it. Drop to the posted work-zone speed and hold it until the zone ends.

Work zone ahead

People are working. Slow down and give them room.

School

1 sign

Five sides — and nothing else on the road has five sides. That shape is how you identify it at a distance, in the dark, or half hidden by a branch. On the road these are posted in fluorescent yellow-green; TN Drive tints school content in its own school color so you can spot it in the app.

School zone

Children cross here. Slow to the posted limit when it's in force.

Review

Railroad

2 signs

Two signs, two jobs. The round yellow sign is a warning — a crossing is coming. The white crossbuck is regulatory: it marks the crossing itself and carries the force of a yield sign. When lights flash or gates lower, stop between 15 and 50 feet from the nearest rail and stay there until the way is clear.

Crossing ahead

Slow, look both ways, be ready to stop.

New Crossbuck

You're at the crossing. Yield to the train.
