Sharing the road · School buses

You are driving on a four-lane road with a center turn lane. A school bus coming toward you stops, flashes its red lights and swings out its stop arm. What must you do?

The rule

Correct

A center turn lane does not divide a highway. Tennessee excuses oncoming traffic from stopping only where the roadway is separated by a physical barrier or an unpaved median — a painted lane is neither. Here, traffic in both directions stops.

The bus driver gives you the signal, not the children. Wait for the arm to fold in and the red lights to go dark, even if the sidewalk looks empty.

“When driving on a highway with separate roadways for traffic in opposite directions, divided by median space or a barrier not suitable for vehicular traffic, the driver need not stop, but should proceed with caution. A turn lane in the middle of a four-lane highway is NOT considered a barrier, but a fifth lane that is suitable for vehicular traffic. Drivers meeting a stopped school bus on this type of road would be required to stop in both directions.”

Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual · Section: Sharing the Road · PDF p. 65
